Small, wet, and louder than you'd think

Frogs have been hopping around for about 250 million years — they watched the dinosaurs come and go. Today more than seven thousand species live on every continent except Antarctica, from rainforest canopies to desert sand.

From egg to frog

Every frog starts life underwater. Most species make the whole journey in a few weeks to a few months.

  1. 01

    Egg

    Laid in water by the hundreds or thousands, each egg is wrapped in jelly.

  2. 02

    Tadpole

    Out swims a tadpole: gills for breathing, a tail for steering, and a taste for algae.

  3. 03

    Froglet

    Legs sprout, lungs develop, and the tail shrinks away. The froglet is a tiny frog with a stub.

  4. 04

    Frog

    The adult climbs out of the water and splits its life between the pond and the land.

A few of the thousands

Every one of them is odd in its own way. Here are five.

  • Red-eyed tree frog

    Sleeps all day with its bright red eyes shut — and flashes them open to startle predators at night. Rainforests of Central America

  • Golden poison frog

    The most poisonous frog on Earth. Its skin toxin was once used to tip hunting darts. Colombian rainforest

  • Glass frog

    Skin so clear on the belly you can watch its heart beat and its organs work. Central and South America

  • Desert rain frog

    A round, grumpy sand-dweller that squeaks like a rubber toy to defend its burrow. Coastal deserts of Namibia and South Africa

  • American bullfrog

    A deep croak that carries across the pond — and an appetite for almost anything that fits in its mouth. Wetlands across North America

Frog facts

Four things worth knowing before you meet one.

  • Skin does the drinking

    Frogs absorb water straight through their belly skin. They never take a sip.

  • Skin does the breathing

    Most frogs take in oxygen through their skin as well as their lungs — which is why they stay damp.

  • Tongue out, catch, back

    The whole trip takes about a tenth of a second. Flies don't stand a chance.

  • Everywhere but Antarctica

    Frogs live on every continent except the frozen one, from rainforests to deserts to back gardens.
